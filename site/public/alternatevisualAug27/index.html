02Whoever walked in

Doors, Not Departments.

The Artist

You can talk about the song in human terms. Tell me what feels wrong and what you are trying not to lose.

The Engineer

The technical depth is there when you want it. Gain structure, impedance interaction, summing, conversion — we can work at that level directly.

The Student

The apprenticeship that no longer exists. If you want to know why a ribbon needs 70 dB of clean gain, I will show you.

The Audiophile

A playback system that never matched the record, taken seriously. A listening room is a room, and rooms can be measured.

The Client

A podcast, a voiceover, a space that has to sound right. Audio work isn’t just for chart-topping hits.

You could be three of these in the same week. The doors route the problem, not the title.

03Why this exists

The Tools Reached Everyone. The Knowledge Didn’t.

For most of a century, knowledge moved through this trade the way signal moves through a patchbay: normalled. It flowed by default — engineer to assistant, assistant to the intern holding the coffee. The rooms were the schools. Nobody called it teaching. It was just how a record got made.

Then the tools got small and cheap and went home with everyone. That part was good. But the power moved faster than the knowledge, and the knowledge was not included in the transfer.

The whole argument, and the patch around the interruption
