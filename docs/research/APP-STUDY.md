# THE APP STUDY — what an app is, what it costs, what it does for ULM

Commissioned by Edward 2026-08-28 ("we need to study how an app can connect
and relate to the detail here as well as the wider lens and grand picture").
Written 2026-08-28. Web-sourced numbers are snapshots accessed that day and
can drift; each carries its source. ULM-specific reasoning traces to repo
documents or Edward's words from the 2026-08-28 session.

The decision this study serves: the SITE becomes a simple companion
statement with two calls to action — (1) find the service, make contact;
(2) take the app for depth. The APP carries the complexity: a free tier
genuinely better than the current workbench toys (Edward's verdict on
those: "simpliton… actually incorrect or really no significant
information" — they may not be the benchmark), and an intense subscription
tier: ongoing lessons, deep wiring/acoustic solutions, AAA-level graphics.

---

## 1 · What an app is good at — and what it is bad at

### Good at (and why it fits ULM)

- **Returning relationships.** Subscription apps retain ~2.5× the
  cross-category median at day 30 (≈14% vs ≈5.4%)¹. An app is a
  possession; a website is a visit. ULM's returning people exist already:
  students mid-course, clients mid-project, engineers who keep a tool.
- **Honest personalization.** The app knows the user's rig, room, project
  and level because the user entered them. This is the exact thing two
  homepage iterations could not honestly fake ("nothing changes for that
  viewer personally" — Edward, on the instrument). The killed
  identity-preset system is, almost verbatim, app onboarding: who are
  you, what's your room, what are you working on.
- **Sensors.** The phone is a measurement instrument: microphone (with
  hard limits, see §3), camera, and on Pro iPhones LiDAR — apps now scan
  a room's geometry and compute modes from it (amcoustics Room Scanner;
  White Mark Toolbox; SonarRoom)². This is native-app territory the web
  cannot reach.
- **Cadence.** Push notifications make lessons a habit loop (practice
  reminders are the spine of Melodics/SoundGym-style products). Web push
  exists on iOS only for installed home-screen apps and opts in 10–15%
  lower than native³.
- **Offline + payments.** Tools that work in a basement with no signal;
  store payment rails that make a $12/mo subscription frictionless.

### Bad at (and where the site stays irreplaceable)

- **Discovery and first contact.** No SEO. Median day-1 retention across
  apps is ~25%, day-30 ~5%¹ — most installs evaporate. An app cannot be
  the front door; a download is commitment, and ULM's funnel thesis is
  zero-commitment entry ("You don't need to know which one it is, or
  what it's called").
- **Freemium economics.** Median download-to-paid in freemium is ≈2.1–
  2.2%; hard paywalls convert ≈5× better (~10–12%) but kill reach⁴. For
  ULM the "loss" is tolerable because the free tier is ALSO marketing —
  but the numbers say: do not plan a livelihood on app revenue alone.
- **The content treadmill.** A subscription must earn its renewal every
  month. For a solo operator whose hours are the actual product, lesson
  content production competes directly with billable work. (Mitigation in
  §5: the curriculum is Edward's existing knowledge written down once —
  the Wiring Bible, the entries "on the bench next" — not a weekly show.)
- **Fleet maintenance.** Two stores, OS updates, review queues, $99/yr
  Apple developer account, refunds/support. Every shipped tool is a
  standing promise.

## 2 · The business side

### Store economics (snapshot 2026-08-28; moving target)

- Apple: 30% standard; **15% Small Business Program** (under $1M/yr —
  ULM qualifies)⁵. US external-payment links currently carry **0%**
  commission under the 2021 injunction; Apple filed 2026-08-13 to charge
  up to 15% (5% for small business) on those — undecided in court⁶.
  Google Play tiers are comparable. Planning number: **15% of app
  revenue goes to the store**, less if external-link pricing survives.

### What comparable products charge (the pricing landscape)

Measurement/acoustics tools:
- **AudioTools** (Studio Six Digital): pro suite, base app plus modules
  from $7.99 to $199.99 (STIPA Pro) — modular one-time purchases⁷.
- **HouseCurve**: free basic frequency-response measurement; room
  correction/filters behind a "Tuning Bundle" — one-year non-renewing
  purchase or lifetime unlock⁸. The closest model to a ULM free/paid
  split, built by a tiny team.
- **SoundID Reference** (Sonarworks): ~$199–299 one-time **with a
  calibrated measurement microphone** in the box⁹ — note the hardware
  bundling as the accuracy answer.
- **SonarRoom** (launched March 2026): AR/3D acoustic room mapping on
  iPhone — "3D room mapping technology once reserved for professional
  studios"². **The AAA-room-acoustics-app lane is now occupied.**
- **White Mark Toolbox**: LiDAR scan, 3D speaker/listener simulation,
  modal prediction — a world-class studio-design firm shipping an app as
  brand + tool². The closest strategic analog to what ULM would be doing.

Education subscriptions:
- **SoundGym**: free tier; ~$24.95/mo, $119/yr, $395 lifetime¹⁰.
- **Melodics**: ~$9.99–20/mo¹⁰.
- **Mix with the Masters**: Pro $319/yr (~$27.90/mo); Student $159/yr¹¹.
- **Puremix**: $89.99/quarter or $269.99/yr¹¹.

The evidence band for audio education is **$10–27/month**, with annual
plans at roughly 10–12× monthly. One-on-one time stays outside the
subscription everywhere — no comparable bundles human hours.

### What the app does TO the core business

- **Proof of knowledge, at scale.** The gap thesis says the knowledge
  stopped being handed down; an app that hands it down is the thesis made
  product. It out-argues any credits page for the audiences who never
  read credits (home engineers, students, congregations).
- **Funnel, both directions.** Site → app (CTA 2) captures people not
  ready to hire; app → services when a tool reaches its honest limit:
  "past this line, the answer isn't a calculation. It's a session" — the
  Workbench's own line, already written. Every deep tool ends at a door.
- **A margin lane that doesn't consume hours** — after the build. Lessons
  content is written once, sold monthly. At the evidence band, 100
  subscribers ≈ $1–2k/mo gross before the 15% store cut — real but
  supplemental; 100 paying subscribers implies thousands of installs at
  freemium conversion rates⁴. Years, not months, of accumulation.
- **Cannibalization check:** recorded lessons compete with the $75–150/hr
  teaching rate (unconfirmed) only at the entry level; historically they
  UPSELL — the comparable platforms all coexist with their founders'
  private-coaching prices. The subscription's ceiling is the reason to
  book the human.
- **Risk, named first: accuracy is the brand.** A tool that reports wrong
  physics under the ULM name damages the only asset that matters. The
  current toys already failed Edward's bench check. LAW carried forward:
  nothing ships a claim Edward has not bench-checked, every tool states
  its own limits, and tools describe what happens — they never predict
  what the user will hear as good (the reason Studio Virtual was shelved
  stays binding in the app).

## 3 · Capability truth table (what each platform can actually do)

| Capability | Web/PWA | Store-wrapped web | Native |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mic input (uncalibrated) | yes (getUserMedia) | yes | yes |
| Calibrated measurement | no realistic path | partial | yes — external-mic profiles, per-device correction |
| DSP/FFT real-time | yes (WebAudio/WASM), adequate for RTA/sweeps | same | best; lowest latency |
| 3D/GPU ceiling | WebGPU now ships on iOS 26 Safari¹² — high, but requires newest OS | same | highest (Metal); no OS-version cliff |
| LiDAR / RoomPlan scan | **no** | **no** | **yes** — the AAA room feature is native-only |
| Push / lesson cadence | installed-PWA only on iOS, weaker opt-in³ | yes | yes |
| Offline | yes (service worker) | yes | yes |
| Background audio | limited on iOS | partial | yes |
| Store presence / IAP rails | no | yes | yes |
| One codebase with the site | **yes** | mostly | no |

**The calibration problem, stated honestly (this shapes the free tier):**
peer-reviewed testing found phone-app SPL readings off by >3 dB in most
apps, with device variability up to ~2.9 dB at 4 kHz; calibrated external
microphones bring error to ±0.5 dB¹³. Consequences: (a) free-tier
measurement tools must say on their face what the built-in mic can and
cannot support (relative measurements, mode-hunting: yes; absolute
verdicts: no); (b) the serious tier pairs with a calibrated mic the way
Sonarworks and Studio Six do — which is also a natural ULM hardware
recommendation/consulting hook; (c) "physics describes; ears decide"
survives into every readout.

## 4 · How far the visuals can go (the AAA question)

- **Web stack (three.js/WebGPU):** with WebGPU default in iOS 26 Safari¹²,
  the browser now does real-time 3D room visualization credibly — but
  only on current-OS devices, and with no LiDAR access. Good enough for:
  animated physics teaching at a level far beyond the current SVG scenes,
  3D mode visualizations of a hand-entered room, signal-path explorers.
- **Flutter (Impeller):** the consensus solo-developer choice for
  custom-drawn, high-frame-rate app UI in 2026¹⁴; full native sensor
  access (mic, LiDAR via platform channels). The realistic chassis for a
  serious cross-platform ULM app.
- **Unity/game engine:** the true AAA ceiling (it powers ~70% of
  top-grossing mobile games¹⁴) and has a native DSP plug-in path; cost is
  a heavier toolchain and app size — justified only if the room becomes a
  walkable 3D world rather than a visualization.
- **Native Swift/Metal:** highest ceiling and best audio latency; doubles
  the codebase count unless iOS-first is accepted (the LiDAR features are
  iPhone-only anyway — an iOS-first flagship is defensible; every LiDAR
  competitor above is iOS-only).
- **The honest constraint is not rendering — it's content.** AAA look is
  achievable solo with AI-assisted development on any of these stacks;
  what cannot be compressed is the acoustics engine's correctness and the
  lesson curriculum. Those are Edward-hours. The stack should be chosen
  to spend Edward-hours on knowledge, not on plumbing.

## 5 · The ULM architecture (recommended)

**Free tier — "the open hand" (the handover, demonstrated):**
- The rebuilt tools at a real standard: bench-checked, honest limit
  statements, visibly beautiful. Candidates: signal-chain/impedance
  advisor (rebuilt from the Wiring Bible with Edward's pass), hum/ground
  diagnostic walk (bench-checked), room-mode explorer from typed room
  dimensions, describing-sound lexicon (borrowed words → mechanisms).
- One lesson thread free, complete, so the teaching is provable.
- Every tool ends with the line between calculation and session — the
  door back to services.

**Subscription — "the apprenticeship" ($10–25/mo band per comps):**
- The lesson paths with cadence (push practice loops), pitched by the
  user's stated identity/level — the preset system reborn where it's honest.
- The deep solvers: guided wiring/grounding resolution, acoustic
  treatment planning with app-grade 3D graphics, measurement workflows
  with calibrated-mic support.
- Subscriber Q&A channel that routes real jobs to the human.

**Flagship, later, native, iOS-first — "the room in your pocket":**
LiDAR scan → 3D room → modes/reflections visualized → treatment plan
drafted → "have Edward review this plan" as the built-in bridge from
subscription to service. The lane SonarRoom/White Mark just opened —
ULM's differentiation is not the scan, it's the thirty years reviewing it.

**The site above all of it:** simple companion statement; services and
identities lightly merged with a glimpse of the detail; two CTAs — contact
/ get the app. Proof, philosophy, and intake stay web forever (SEO,
zero-commitment entry, the personal reply).

## 6 · Path, gates, risks

- **Phase 0 — definition (now, cheap):** name the free tools and their
  accuracy statements; Edward bench-checks the physics; pick the first
  lesson thread; decide the app's name/mark. Gate: Edward can say in one
  sentence what the free app does for a stranger.
- **Phase 1 — the free companion (web-tech, installable, one codebase
  with the site):** rebuilt tools + one lesson thread + the two-CTA site
  shipping together. No store presence yet; "add to home screen" +
  QR-from-site. Gate to phase 2: real usage returning (people coming
  back unprompted), tools surviving contact with users.
- **Phase 2 — the store app + subscription (Flutter or native shell):**
  store presence, push cadence, paid tier. Requires: content backlog for
  ≥6 months of subscriber value, support plan, pricing confirmed by
  Edward (rates law). Gate: subscription covers its own maintenance.
- **Phase 3 — the native flagship (LiDAR room).** Only after phase 2
  sustains; iOS-first; possibly its own price.
- **Risks, ranked:** 1) accuracy/credibility (mitigated by bench-check
  law); 2) the content treadmill (mitigated by written-once curriculum
  from existing material); 3) competitive window (SonarRoom et al. move
  fast; ULM's moat is the human behind the tool, not the tool); 4) scope
  creep back into the website (mitigated by the two-CTA covenant: the
  site stays simple, forever).
