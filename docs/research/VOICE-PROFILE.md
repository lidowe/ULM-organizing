## B. Verdict patterns

### What he rejects (with the actual case)

1. **Assertion without image — "ambiguous AI slop."** Flagged by him/the
   audit: "we provide the means and the message", "We understand the
   frustration", "we believe in empowering the user", "so all of us get to
   better creative output", "Wherever you are in your journey" ("journey" =
   the one marketing word in an otherwise concrete block). The test the audit
   distilled from his reactions: *no sentence without an image in it survives.*
2. **Predatory reads.** "Send Me The Song" trashed everywhere — reads as
   "send me something so I can steal your IP." Budget/timeline fields at the
   top of a form read as screening; moved to one optional note at the end.
3. **Upgraded claims.** Never inflate a credit; never place a marquee name
   where it reads as one; the disputed 103-mic count came out of the hero —
   "the page's job is judgment, not inventory." He has consistently chosen
   the more conservative wording when asked.
4. **Decoration.** Puns that were only decoration got dropped; puns carrying
   information stayed. Icons were "supposed to be tiny little side bars."
   Motion only as narration. A colour never moonlights as decoration.
5. **Repetition/inflation of the same thought.** "reached the proper level,
   took the elevator up and found your level in two consecutive sentences" —
   cut. Mission told four times across pages — flagged as the source of
   incoherence.
6. **Predicted judgment.** The Studio Virtual app was shelved because it
   forecast how things would sound: "physics describes, ears decide." Copy
   states what happens (ratio, bandwidth, noise), offers the ear test, and
   never forecasts the verdict.
7. **Handoff/baton metaphors.** A baton is work moving down a line to the
   next station — the thing the site argues against.
8. **Theorizing over his ranking.** The whole research corpus: "a map with no
   legend." Open-ended strategizing without his ordering leads "miles off
   course."
9. **Old copy seeping back** ("you must be conflating the old lovable site
   again"), word-by-word wording ping-pong (wants one numbered batch), and
   the question/options UI (it has eaten his input).
10. **Talent trimmed for commerce** — the taste-level version of all of the
    above: Green Day "trimmed away a lot of what wouldn't be commercially
    viable, and I don't like that."

### What he keeps / praises

- Subtraction: In Rainbows is the north star — "how much you leave out."
- Specific, wry, credentialed by detail — coffee-to-console, the Telefunken
  story, the purple guitar. Detail that proves the author was in the room.
- Honesty of capture over cosmetics: the 311 kick, "no tricks", level-matched
  and labeled as level-matched.
- Puns that carry information; "Accidentals carries both senses at once."
- Terms said out loud: unsponsored, prices on the page, independent budgets
  real, criticisms explicitly welcome, "there is nobody else answering."
- The small job dignified: "Major project or minor tweak" — the line that
  tells someone with a two-minute job they are allowed to be here.
- Trades named honestly: remote loses something and he says so.

## C. His humor

Deadpan, specific, self-aware about studio culture, and always resolving to
generosity. Mechanisms observed:

1. **The priorities inversion.** Telefunken: reassure about the gear first
   ("Don't worry, the mic wasn't damaged."), beat of ellipsis, then the human
   ("… Oh, he was checked by a doctor and was fine too."), then the
   deadpan-generous closer ("We gave him the rest of the night off."). The
   joke is that studio culture really does think in that order, and he knows
   it.
2. **The parenthetical straight-face.** Mid-story technical aside delivered
   flat: "(Tube equipment is very high voltage)". The fact IS the punchline
   support.
3. **Setup that sounds like praise, then the reversal.** "A production line
   never misses a beat. That's what's wrong with it."
4. **Q&A with himself, pun as payoff.** "Wondering why the 3rd degree? Well,
   we're trying to establish the Root."
5. **Borrowed absurdism, credited.** "In order to make a grilled cheese,
   first you must create the universe." — paraphrasing Carl Sagan — then he
   plays it completely straight as process description.
6. **Self-deprecating credential.** "…I'm asked to build their private studio
   after our work together commercially… by being their barista a decade
   earlier." · "yes, even the coffee can ruin an entire day, or fuel the
   magic later."
7. **Letting others swear for him.** "Some people put it less politely, and
   that has been said too: just f**king do it" — reported speech, asterisks
   in, register intact.
8. **Visual deadpan.** His photo associations: the bassist in a bear suit
   placed beside "it is the artist expressing their emotion, their story."
   In the icon grammar: the VU needle works but "never past halfway — that is
   the joke."
9. Never sarcasm at a person; the target is systems, gear folklore, or
   himself. "There are no villains in it."

## E. Sentence mechanics (observed in his verbatim copy)

1. **Clause-chaining by comma.** He runs thoughts together where speech would:
   "It is no longer a huge intimidating console behind soundlocked doors, the
   console can exist in the palm of your hand, but the creative use… was left
   behind." Sessions that "fixed" this were ordered to restore it verbatim
   (commit 34d2e29: contractions expanded, ellipses turned to commas, run-ons
   split — all reverted).
2. **Ellipsis as a timing beat, not a trail-off.** "The takeaway is … no
   matter the task" · "Sound… is described in borrowed words" · "the proper
   scale … without accidentals" · the beat before "Oh, he was checked by a
   doctor." It is a comedic/musical rest.
3. **Long build, short close.** Multi-clause sentences resolved by a 4–8 word
   declarative: "We gave him the rest of the night off." · "That's what's
   wrong with it." · "We've got you." · "That is fine too."
4. **Second person, early and often.** The reader is "you" with a project,
   not "artists." Questions addressed at the reader that he then answers:
   "Doesn't it feel like 90%…?" · "and you're expected to perform and be
   documented forever?"
5. **Parenthetical fact-asides.** "(Tube equipment is very high voltage)" ·
   "(the Roland Dimension D)" · "(the Eventide H3500 being his favorite)".
   The parenthesis holds the spec so the sentence keeps its stride.
6. **Precise nouns as texture**: $15,000 Telefunken Elam 251, 70 dB of clean
   gain, 1176, dbx 160VU, hysteresis, windings and blends of metal. Numbers
   and model names are how he proves presence.
7. **Additive role lists**: "musician, recording engineer, mixer, producer,
   studio owner and manager, acoustic consultant, and university instructor" —
   he catalogues rather than summarizes.
8. **Contractions and informality**: "don't stress", "it's", "thru", "pretty
   interesting", scare quotes around industry words: a 'hit', your 'brand' or
   'image'.
9. **The reassurance reflex**: "And don't stress" · "Don't worry" · "that is
   fine too" · "there is no harm in asking" · "You don't need to know what
   it's called." He lowers the barrier in nearly every unit.
10. **Both-directions honesty in one sentence**: "even the coffee can ruin an
    entire day, or fuel the magic later" · "Nothing fully replaces being in
    the same space. We trade some of that for access, and we are honest about
    it."
11. **Aphorism closers**, plainly worded, no flourish: "It is better to
    listen to the artist in order to hear the song." · "Performance first.
    Tuning is a finishing decision, not a rescue."
12. **I/we**: "I" when Edward tells his own story (About, stories, teaching
    offers); "we" when ULM speaks about the work. (Drift exists mid-page; the
    audit proposed this as the rule.)
13. **Headings**: every word capitalized ("The Industry Is Undergoing Major
    Key Changes." / "What Is Getting In The Way?"), often full sentences with
    the period kept, sometimes a question he answers below.

## F. "Does this sentence sound like Edward?" — 15 concrete tests

1. **Image test**: does the sentence contain a thing you could photograph or
   patch into (a room, a mic, a fader, a car, a coffee)? "Means and the
   message" fails; "the mic zapped him thru the booth wall" passes.
2. **Double-duty test**: if it uses a music word (key, scale, tempo, root,
   resolve, beat), does it work in BOTH senses at once? A pun that only
   decorates gets cut.
3. **Liner-notes test**: could every credit/claim be defended to someone
   holding the liner notes? No upgraded roles, no marquee proximity.
4. **Verdict test**: does it describe what happens (mechanism, spec, fact)
   rather than predict what the reader will hear or feel as good? "Physics
   describes, ears decide."
5. **Spec-as-texture test**: is there at least one exact noun or number where
   a generic would fit — 70 dB, Elam 251, dbx 160VU — and is that spec true?
   (One wrong gear fact = "years wasted in a flash.")
6. **Barrier test**: does the sentence lower the door for the small job
   ("Major project or minor tweak", "that is fine too") rather than qualify
   or screen the reader?
7. **Take-something-out test** (In Rainbows): remove the weakest clause; if
   the sentence survives, ship it without the clause. "…exists to solve, so
   all of us get to better creative output" → end at "solve."
8. **Hedge count**: at most zero hedges. "I will do my best to promptly
   respond to any topic of interest we may have missed" hedges three times;
   his register is "Messages come to me and get answered — there is nobody
   else here to answer them."
9. **Predator test**: read it as a scam-burned artist would. Does it ask them
   to hand something over before it gives anything? ("Send Me The Song"
   failed this.)
10. **Villain test**: if it criticizes, does it name systems and habits, never
    people or companies? ("There are no villains in it." / grievance is
    diagnosis, not complaint.)
11. **Reported-speech test**: does color (swearing, hype, client language)
    arrive quoted from someone else — "just f**king do it", "make it more
    purple" — rather than as his own register?
12. **Rest-of-the-night test**: does the humor stay deadpan, resolve kindly,
    and target gear culture or himself, never the reader?
13. **Second-person test**: is the reader "you" with a specific problem ("a
    mix that will not sit", "the record you are trying to finish"), not an
    audience segment?
14. **Punctuation fingerprint**: comma-chained clauses allowed; an ellipsis
    used as a rest is a good sign; a tidy semicolon where he'd run on is a
    tell of house style. Never "correct" his rhythm.
15. **Both-sides test**: when the sentence sells a strength, does it also name
    the trade? ("We trade some of that for access, and we are honest about
    it.") A claim with no cost attached is not his.

### Non-negotiables when writing new copy

- Draft in his direction, deliver as a **numbered veto list**; nothing is
  final until his numbers come back. Never invent copy in his voice and call
  it done.
- Never rewrite a sentence that is his; mechanical fixes only (typos,
  entities, casing).
- Headings: every word capitalized, written into the copy, and "Major" keeps
  its capital for the pun.
- No technical claim unless it is his sentence quoted or a number computed
  from his data.
