# Upper Level Music — site brief

The constraint document for the rebuild. Written from three strategy documents,
two long planning conversations, and the current build. Everything here is a
decision already made or an open question clearly marked as one.

Read this before writing copy, structure, or code. If something on the site
can't be traced back to a line in here, it probably shouldn't be there.

---

## 1. The one job

Close the gap between the result someone intended and the result they got —
wherever that gap lives.

Every service is that job at a different point. Mixing is it at the mix stage.
Teaching is it at the skill stage. A room diagnosis is it at the monitoring
stage. This is what lets the range of work read as one practice rather than a
man who does everything.

**Ed's own line, already written and better than any replacement:** *"I get
hired to fill a gap, and the gap is different every time."*

---

## 2. The four gates

Every visitor clears four questions, in order, and stalls at whichever is
hardest for them. Page weight goes where the stalls concentrate — not where the
material is most interesting to us.

| Gate | Question | Who stalls here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is this for someone like me? | Home producers (*will this condescend to me*), utility audio (*does he even do this*) |
| 2 | Is this person any good? | Funded artists (*is he current and serious*), peer engineers (*does he actually know things*) |
| 3 | What can I actually get? | **Everyone.** The universal bottleneck. |
| 4 | What happens if I move? | Independents (*what does it cost*), home producers (*will this be embarrassing*) |

### The rule that follows

**Where two audiences need opposite things at the same gate, prose can't serve
both — only a signal can.** Prose has to pick one reader. A signal carries two
readings at once.

- **Gate 1 → signals, not sentences.** The needs here are genuinely opposite
  (*serious operator* vs *won't condescend*). Two signals already exist and both
  work: the musical pun in the hero, and the rack photo. The pun reads
  *musician* to a musician and as ordinary language to everyone else. The rack
  reads *he knows the technical* to an engineer and *professional* to everyone
  else. Neither costs the other audience a word.
- **Gate 2 → distributed into the doors.** Credibility evidence is
  audience-specific by nature — plaques convince the funded artist and mean
  nothing to the home producer; topology reasoning convinces the peer and
  repels the artist. It therefore cannot stack at the entrance.
- **Gate 3 → maximum weight, earliest.** The shared bottleneck gets the most
  page. Three doors, plainly labelled.
- **Gate 4 → one intake, published range.**

People scan headings and skip the prose between them. **Gate 3 must be
answerable from headings alone** — the doors have to work with the body copy
unread.

Root → three doors → depth as proof. Nav is three doors plus one action.

**Dissolves:** the standalone services price menu (rates redistribute into the
doors — a commodity menu at the root betrays the positioning), the second

**The three doors already exist**, written in Ed's voice, buried on `/process`
behind accordions: *"Just f\*\*king do it." / "Help me be my best, coach me
through it." / "Teach me, I'm going pro."* They get promoted to the spine. Their
public labels are still open — see §7.

## 4. The manifesto, distributed

**There is no manifesto page.** The argument is true and it is load-bearing, but
stated as a section it reads as a rant of gripes, and leading with what's wrong
makes the author a jaded hasbeen pretending to be the cure.

A fragment earns its place when it **explains a choice the visitor is already
looking at.** Attached to a decision it's reasoning; floating free it's
complaint.

| Fragment (Ed's raw words — to be edited, not pasted) | Where it goes | The choice it explains |
|---|---|---|
| Access got democratized. Judgment didn't. | Root, near the doors | Why there are three doors instead of a service list |
| Finishing a good product is easy. Finishing your passion project isn't. | Top of **Hire** | What you're actually buying |
| No weekly video, no class, no training data, and nobody here talking about someone else's project. | Top of **Learn** | Why this isn't a course |
| Everyone who could tell you says luck, support, attitude — the honest version costs them and the script doesn't. | **Bring me a problem** | Why pay someone with no stake in the answer |
| The engineer absorbs the rescue and gets no points, no residuals, no stake either way. | Next to **pricing** | Why the conversation is priced, and why there's no free sample |
| The tools let you get the part right in two takes instead of two hundred. Some had it pushed on them, some walked through the door because it opened. | **About** | Why the practice exists |
| Get it right at the source. A decision made early costs nothing; a rescue made later costs everything. | **Process / preproduction** | Why he asks about rehearsal before booking |
| I own a lot of gear for the same dopamine reason anyone does. I just know what's being marketed to me. | **Studio / gear depth** | Why the locker isn't a flex |

The fourth "manifesto door" is optional and small. If the distribution works,
it may not be needed at all.

## 5. Voice

Four rules, all derived from what worked and what failed in drafting.

1. **Check the grammatical subject of every sentence.** If the reader can be
   the subject of a failure, rewrite until the subject is the extractors, the
   structure, or the work. *"Everything the home engineer knows is based on
   bullshit"* loses the segment you most want. *"The door opened and people
   walked through"* accuses nobody and lands the same point.
2. **Structural, not moral.** The two sharpest observations both explain a bad
   outcome through incentives rather than villainy — nobody is lazy, nobody is
   a fraud. That register survives being read the next morning. Moral framing
   doesn't.
3. **Implicate yourself immediately after any hard observation.** Ten guitars
   that never played a show → *yes, that's part of why I own so much gear.*
   This is what makes brutal material readable instead of sneering, and it is
   also the standing: not above the system, in the same water, just able to
   read the label.
4. **Every block is a proof, an action, or a demonstration.** If it's none of
   those it's a statement about yourself — which is where all the swelling
   comes from. Cut it.

**On "I" vs "we":** Ed is the practice; roughly 95% of work is his. Contributors
are real, values-selected, and exist partly to cover what remote can't — someone
trusted who can physically be in the room. Frame the network as vouched-for
people, never as a roster. *"A focused team working for you"* is the agency read
and walks toward the commodity pole. The named human is the defensible position.

## 6. Standing rules

- **Visible incompleteness is deliberate.** The site is built in public across
  sessions. Placeholder sections that say so in their own voice stay.
- **But editorial to-dos are not transparency.** Eight `needs-content` blocks
  have shipped publicly more than once — including one telling visitors every
  price was a working draft and one calling the client roster "largely
  unverifiable." *"The first posts are on the way"* belongs on the page.
  *"Confirm the hourly rate before publishing"* is a note to self that leaked.
- **Credits are lineage, not recency.** Public credits stop around 2014 by
  choice; recent work is largely uncredited by design. The old credits prove
  training inside a system that no longer exists — which is the argument. They
  are not a claim to be a current chart mixer.
- **No outcome promises.** The job is done when the work is good. Revenue,
  streams and audience are explicitly not offered — no promotion services, no
  career claims.
- **Say when someone doesn't need you.** This is not a nice touch; it's the
  proof the critique isn't self-serving, and it's credible because it's true.
- **Depth exists but waits.** Technical specificity is the least fakeable
  asset. It signals up front through imagery; it pays off behind a door.

## 7. Open decisions

Not guesses. These block work and only Ed can settle them.

1. **The price of the conversation.** Length, price, and whether anything is
   delivered afterward. A written follow-up — *here's what I heard, here's the
   gap, here's what I'd do* — is the difference between a thing they keep and
   a chat they paid for.
2. **The public labels for the three doors.** The internal versions are right;
   the visitor-facing wording is the only navigation a person gets.
3. **Whether "Bring me a problem" is the visual centre**, or whether Hire leads.
   This decides what Ed is known as, which is a business question, not a design
   one.

## 8. Where this came from

Three strategy documents, two long planning conversations, and a read of the
current build. Two of those documents cite each other and this conversation as
sources — treat their agreement as circularity, not corroboration.

The strongest material in all of it came out of Ed under questioning, not out
of any research: comping culture and what it replaced, why practitioners can't
afford to say true things publicly, and engineers absorbing rescue work with no
ownership stake. None of that appears in any of the documents, because no agent
could have found it.
