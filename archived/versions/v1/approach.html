Approach

The chain serves the record.

I do not choose equipment by brand mythology. I choose the path for the source, the performance, and the record’s intent.

A microphone, preamp, cable, compressor, EQ, converter, and monitor path are not separate boxes. In practice they behave as one system.

That matters because the system changes what an artist hears, how they perform, and how the listener receives the record. The same vocal can feel immediate, restrained, expensive, aggressive, fragile, dense, or exposed depending on where the chain loads, where it gives, and where it stays out of the way.

The goal is not technical display. The goal is to choose the circuit path that lets the artist remain recognizable while the record becomes more convincing.

This is where engineering and taste meet: the science is real, but it is used as part of the art.

Working principles

Specific without turning into a manual.

Capture sets the emotional frame.

Most of the record’s eventual tone is committed before the mix. Mic placement, source level, impedance relationships, and gain staging decide whether the performance already feels like the record or has to be rescued later.

Specs matter, but pairings matter more.

A device does not exist alone. The source, input, output, transformer behavior, cable, headroom, and receiving stage form a composite decision. The useful question is not “what does this box sound like?” It is “what does this chain make this artist feel like?”

Width, density, and translation are linked.

Stereo image choices affect mono compatibility, vocal focus, low-end stability, and emotional scale. Space is not added because it is impressive. It is added when it helps the song carry the feeling without falling apart on smaller playback systems.

Signal flow is part of production.

Routing, patching, gain structure, and monitoring decisions shape what gets played, what gets kept, and what the artist trusts. A good setup disappears; a poor one forces the performance to fight the room.
