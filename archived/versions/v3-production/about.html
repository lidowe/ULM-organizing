Edward Lidow

I came to engineering from the artist side.

Musician first. Independent records before the major rooms. Hit Factory / Criteria is where that background was turned into professional range.

The job changed. The reason for doing it didn't.

I spent high school and college in bands and was making independent records before engineering became the profession. That history still affects how I work. The person in front of the microphone is not a “source.” The record is not a technical exercise. Somebody is trying to put something of themselves into a form another person can feel.

I moved through media and formal audio training at SAE Institute Miami, graduating valedictorian, then into Hit Factory / Criteria. I spent twelve years there. The studio system, the engineers around it, and the volume and variety of major sessions turned a broad musical background into the ability to move between roles without letting the room slow down.

That is where I was honed as a professional: large-format rooms, major-label expectations, different genres, producers, artists, deadlines, and the need to solve the next problem without disturbing the creative process.

Formation

The route to Upper Level Music.

Musician first

Bands, songs, independent records.

High school and college bands came before studio engineering as a career. Making indie records taught the artist-side reality first: budgets, indecision, ambition, bad language for good instincts, and the distance between the music in your head and the record coming back through the speakers.

Miami

Media, SAE, and formal engineering.

Work in media led deeper into audio. Edward completed formal training at SAE Institute Miami as valedictorian, adding the technical framework to experience that had started in rehearsal rooms and independent sessions.

12 years

Hit Factory / Criteria.

Twelve years inside a major studio system, running and assisting sessions across hip-hop, pop, Latin, country, and rock. The education was as much about people, pace, preparation, and session judgment as it was consoles and equipment.

Freelance

Different rooms. Different roles.

That range carried into freelance work across rooms including Record Plant Los Angeles, Chicago Recording Company, Dream Asylum, and others; founding and later selling Studio 8 Miami; and taking on the engineering, editing, production, mixing, and technical roles a project actually needed.

Now

Upper Level Music · Columbia, SC.

Upper Level Music is Edward's private studio and working base in Columbia, South Carolina. He also teaches at Midlands Audio Institute, passing on the apprenticeship-style context that shaped his own professional development.

Industry awareness without sanding off the reason you made the song.

The major-label years gave me a practical understanding of what helps records function at scale: pace, translation, format expectations, competitive context, and the decisions that can keep a project viable.

That perspective is useful. It is not a default aesthetic. If your goal is a competitive single, I know how to think about the record in that context. If your goal is something stranger, more personal, or deliberately outside the middle, I do not force it toward “sellable” just to demonstrate that I know the rules.

You are the person expressing yourself through the music. My role is to give you the range, judgment, and technical support to bring that expression into the real world at the highest standard I can.
