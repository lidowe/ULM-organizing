The artist stays in the center.

The first job is to understand what you are trying to express. The production, performance, recording, editing, and mix should make that clearer—not replace it with somebody else's idea of what your music ought to be.

I know how records are expected to function at a major-label level. That perspective is available when it helps. It is not a template I impose on the song.

If the goal is a competitive single, we can think that way. If the goal is something stranger, narrower, heavier, quieter, or more personal, the same professional discipline still applies.

Musical instinct · technical fluency

The electronics matter. They just aren't the subject.

Different microphones, circuits, gain stages, processors, and converters react differently to a voice, an instrument, and to each other. I use that knowledge the way a musician uses touch, arrangement, or tone: to support the character of the performance.

You do not need to know the terminology. If you are a producer or engineer and do, we can go as deep as the problem requires.
