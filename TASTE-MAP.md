# Edward's taste map — calibration case file (16 Aug 2026)

Distilled from Edward's own annotated listening answer. His words in quotes.
This is the layer every design, copy, and (eventually) audio judgment
calibrates against. Not site content.

## The organizing principle

**His genre is Talent.** "If I can hear talent, then I like it" — musical,
engineering, production, even marketing/branding talent (Taylor Swift
respected-not-listened-to for team loyalty, craft, and the Eras bar).
Corollary with teeth: talent withheld or trimmed for commerce is the sin
(Green Day "trimmed away a lot of what wouldn't be commercially viable, and
I don't like that"); self-aware absurdity is forgiven (latter-day Weezer).

## The north star: In Rainbows

"It's not about how much you put into a song, but how much you leave out…
simple, beautiful, sparse, poetic, vulnerable… not a single extra note
wasted." The subtraction opus. **Every page of the site is held to this
test.** His earlier critiques ("busy", "when everything is loud, nothing
is") are this principle applied.

## The role model: Nigel Godrich

"He knew how to navigate it all without following anyone, without giving up
his principles, and with total mastery." When Edward appears on the site,
he occupies Nigel's chair: core of the band, never the star.

## Production ethics: the 311 kick drum

"You hear the kick drum and exactly how hard Chad works… no sampled kick,
no tricks… they play music and leave their talent in the product."
Honesty of capture over cosmetics. Governs the fader demo (level-matched,
labeled) and all audio claims.

## The Radiohead arc (how he reads catalogs — and himself)

Master the form (The Bends → OK Computer, the album "that nearly broke
them") → pivot before stagnation, failures kept private (Kid A/Amnesiac) →
prove both at once (Hail to the Thief — his most-listened) → the
subtraction opus (In Rainbows) → single-variable exploration (rhythm:
King of Limbs; orchestration: Moon Shaped Pool) → recombine, then step back
to individual work. He values catalog-as-project: bands "running their band
from idea to completion of architecture."

## Bands and what each one certifies

- **QOTSA** — opposing forces held together: "rock your face off" AND
  "texture and arrangements of beauty." Contrast as craft.
- **311** — identity constancy ("still the same band since high school…
  never chased hits"), genre fusion as unity-of-talents, lyrical arc he
  identifies with (angst → vices → missing home → Sand Dollars: "we don't
  know if we're chasing the dollar or the music"), player virtuosity (Tim's
  five guitar personalities, P-Nut, Chad's un-tricked dynamics).
- **Audioslave** — great mixes; Cornell "a master of rock vocals."
- **Fleet Foxes** — transportive detail: "makes you feel like you're on top
  of a snowy tree-filled mountain breathing fresh air." Music as place.
- **Nirvana / early Weezer / early Green Day** — formative; loyalty is to
  the honest era.
- Wilco, Sublime, Kendrick, No Doubt (until the breakup), classic rock at
  large.

## Working references (mixing)

- **No Doubt, Rock Steady** — reference mix; "Love Spike Stent." Stent-era
  **Muse** likewise.
- **Lil Wayne ft. T-Pain, "Got Money"** — low-end reference for rap: "I was
  there and I did half the work… Fabian's mixing showed me what bass and
  low end is meant to be in rap." He can deconstruct it because he knows
  the process underneath.
- **Modern pop** — referenced for mix craft: "empty from the musicianship
  side but how full and great the mixing is — that's the talent in pop."
- **Tom Lord-Alge over Chris** — "Tom is the better engineer. Peak 90s.
  Live would never have had success without his mix." No exit.
- **Dangermouse** — "weaves a great palette." **Stewart Copeland** —
  favorite drummer.

## The origin story (site-worthy someday, his words, barely edited)

Heard a song in Forrest Gump as a kid, thought it was the Doors — it was
Hendrix. Bought the Doors album anyway, loved it, then chased the entire
history of recorded music through every genre — the Beatles through Motown
through the 70s, the 80s, every local scene, everything before and after he
was born — "until it all homogenized into the industry and the top-down
thing." Now: the same ten bands since his teens, growing with the ones that
survive.

## One honest datum

He doesn't know the credits of most of what he loves. His taste is heard,
not read. For the site that sells ears, that is a feature — it keeps the
authority claim on listening, not on trivia.
